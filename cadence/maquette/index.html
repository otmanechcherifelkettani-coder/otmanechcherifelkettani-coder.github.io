Expertise

My expertise in 3 questions

01

Where is the value?

I start with the business, not the technology.

I identify where the company is losing time, money, capacity, quality or revenue, then trace those problems back to the workflows, systems and decisions that create them.

What matters?Where are the biggest constraints?Where is value being lost?

02

What is worth doing?

Not every problem needs AI.

For each opportunity, I determine the simplest intervention that can materially improve the economics of the work.

Process redesignAutomationAISoftwareThird-party solutionHuman intervention

The objective isn't the most advanced technology.

It's knowing when technology is worth using — and making it work.

From opportunity→ to working system→ to measurable impact.

The methodology

One value-first approach, from opportunity to impact.

01Ambition

Define the ambition

Start with the outcome.

What business result are we trying to improve?

CostRevenueSpeedQualityCapacityCustomer experienceRisk

02Reality

Understand the operating reality

Understand how the work actually happens today.

Map the workflows, systems, people, decisions and constraints shaping the outcome.

OutputCurrent-state view of the operation.
03Constraints

Identify the constraints

Find where value is being lost. Look for:

Manual effortBottlenecksWaitingRepetitive workKnowledge gapsErrorsPoor decisionsHandoffsFragmented systemsException handling

Then identify the underlying cause.

OutputHigh-value opportunity areas.

Ask where technology could materially change the way the work gets done.

"Where can we use AI?"

"Where could technology create a fundamentally better way of producing this outcome?"

OutputDefined opportunities with a clear value hypothesis.

Scale·Iterate·Redesign·Stop

01Uber Eats

Rebuilding customer support around LLMs

Uber Eats had a fragmented post-order support experience built around legacy intent detection and workflows.

The opportunity wasn't simply to "add AI."

I helped lead the global rebuild of the experience around an LLM-based architecture, from identifying the opportunity and redesigning the workflow through to product, engineering and operations execution.

The lessonThe value came from redesigning the workflow around the technology — not simply introducing an LLM.

02Cars24

Using AI to reshape the sales process

Cars24 deployed AI voice and chat agents across buying, selling, financing and follow-up, starting in parts of the funnel where customer conversations directly influence conversion.

The company also expanded AI internally across engineering, finance, legal, marketing and operations, with around 600 employees using ChatGPT Enterprise and Codex; OpenAI reports 85–90% daily active usage among that group.

The interesting part is not simply that Cars24 deployed AI. It embedded AI into a revenue-generating workflow, changing how customer interactions were handled across the funnel.

The lessonSome of the highest-value opportunities are not about automating a task. They are about changing how an entire commercial workflow operates.

03Procurement

AI uncovering 20–29% savings opportunities

McKinsey describes a technology company using linked AI agents to rethink external-services procurement.

The system combined spend and market data, identified pricing trends and savings opportunities, and modelled demand under different market scenarios to support sourcing decisions.

Procurement is not traditionally viewed as an "AI department." The opportunity emerged from examining the economics of the workflow and identifying where AI could materially improve spend analysis, market intelligence, demand modelling and supplier decisions.

The lessonSome of the biggest technology opportunities may be hiding in parts of the business that don't look "AI-native" at all.
